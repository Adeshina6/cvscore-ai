🤖 What is ATS?
An ATS is software that most medium and large companies use to collect, sort, and filter job applications before a human recruiter ever reads them. When you apply online, your resume is typically parsed by an ATS first. If it doesn't meet the system's keyword and formatting requirements, it gets filtered out automatically — regardless of how qualified you are.
The vast majority of companies with more than 50 employees use some form of ATS. Systems like Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, and Taleo are industry-standard. If you've ever applied through a company's career portal or LinkedIn Easy Apply, your resume was almost certainly processed by an ATS.
Three common reasons: (1) Your resume uses formatting an ATS can't parse — multi-column layouts, text boxes, tables, or images. (2) You're missing the exact keywords the job description uses, even if you have the underlying skills. (3) Your contact information or work history dates aren't in a format the ATS expects. CVScore AI diagnoses all three in under 60 seconds.
📊 Scanning & scores
The score combines six weighted dimensions: parseability (can a machine extract your data reliably?), formatting (is the layout ATS-safe?), keyword match (how many of the job description's key terms appear?), readability (are your bullets strong and specific?), skills alignment (do your listed skills map to the role?), and contact info accuracy. The weights are calibrated to reflect what real ATS systems prioritize.
A score above 75 puts you in good shape for most ATS filters. Scores above 85 are considered strong matches. Anything below 60 typically means formatting or keyword issues that will prevent your resume from reaching a human reader. The score is most meaningful when paired with a specific job description — without one, it reflects general ATS readiness rather than job-specific match.

📁 Files & formats
We accept PDF and DOCX files up to 5 MB. These are the two formats real ATS systems process, so testing your resume in both formats is a good idea. If your resume is in another format (Pages, Google Docs, ODT), export it to PDF or DOCX first.
It depends on the ATS the company uses. DOCX files are more reliably parsed by older ATS platforms (Taleo, iCIMS). PDFs are safer for newer platforms (Greenhouse, Lever) and preserve your visual formatting. When in doubt, apply with DOCX unless the job posting specifies PDF. CVScore AI tests both so you know how each version performs.
The most common culprits: two- or three-column layouts (the ATS reads columns left-to-right across the page, mangling your content), tables used for skills or experience, text inside text boxes or headers/footers, images containing text, and non-standard section headings. CVScore AI flags every one of these specifically — not just "formatting issues exist."
